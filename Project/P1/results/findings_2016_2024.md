# Abstention, selection and the Blue Wall: findings

**Scope:** CES + COVI; separately fitted 2016 and 2024 cross sections. Samples: 32,453 and 31,484, including 5,539 and 2,185 matched nonvoters.

**Allocation assumption:** Every predicted non-Republican preference is assigned to Democrats in the headline scenario. The model outcome remains Republican versus all other candidates. This is not measured Democratic support.

## National findings

| Allocated Democratic preference | 2016: estimate (95% CI) | 2024: estimate (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline, state controls | 58.6% (56.0-61.2) | 55.4% (51.3-59.6) |
| Joint binary selection, COVI | 69.8% (40.0-99.5) | 84.8% (72.1-97.5) |

Intervals are model-based, with state-clustered fitting and covariate-mix uncertainty.

- The baseline allocated Democratic share falls from 58.6% to 55.4%. The 3.15 percentage-point decline is not significant at 5% (state-clustered p = 0.077).
- The selection model instead predicts 69.8% and 84.8%. Its cross-year change is also not significant (p = 0.368); the 2016 interval is especially wide.
- Among retained respondents reporting a previous Democratic vote, abstention versus a current Republican vote is 17.7% versus 7.9% in 2016, and 4.1% versus 3.9% in 2024. These are weighted within-sample descriptions, not national voter-flow totals.
- The data are more compatible with abstention exceeding defection among prior Democratic voters in 2016 than in 2024. They do not establish either mechanism as the cause of an election result.
- Error correlation: rho = 0.346 in 2016 (p = 0.488) and 0.826 in 2024 (p = 0.001). COVI turnout association: p = 0.001 in 2016; p = 0.128 in 2024. Exclusion validity remains assumed.
- For 2024 nonvoters with a usable pre-election preference, 45.6% stated Republican preference. On that same subset, baseline prediction is 44.1% and selection prediction is 16.0%. The large selection adjustment conflicts with this survey benchmark.

## State participation thresholds

Additional participants required to exceed the Republican margin, with all existing votes fixed and all non-Republican preferences allocated to Democrats. These are expected-net-vote calculations, not actual additional ballots observed.

| Year / state | Trump lead | Baseline: added voters | Selection: added voters | NV n / Kish n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 / MI | 10,704 | 63,667 | 26,644 | 205 / 79.6 |
| 2016 / WI | 22,748 | 121,493 | 57,107 | 118 / 59.1 |
| 2016 / PA | 44,292 | 475,572 | 119,100 | 292 / 154.1 |
| 2024 / MI | 80,103 | No net D gain | 134,397 | 87 / 13.4 |
| 2024 / WI | 29,397 | 132,627 | 41,983 | 27 / 6.4 |
| 2024 / PA | 120,266 | 731,370 | 166,645 | 73 / 7.8 |

- Michigan 2024 is almost evenly divided in the baseline: allocated Democratic preference is 49.9%. Adding participants with that point-estimated mix does not overturn its Republican lead. The selection model gives a different scenario; all three states cannot be claimed to flip from the baseline estimates.
- Allocation sensitivity is consequential. In Pennsylvania 2016, the baseline needs more than 83.0% of non-Republicans to vote Democratic just to produce a positive Democratic net gain. Allocating only 80% produces no positive net gain there, however many participants are added.
- If all three states flipped, standard state allocations would give Democrats 278 electoral votes in 2016 (before faithless electors) and 270 in 2024, holding every other state fixed. These are arithmetic scenarios, not estimated probabilities of winning.
- The 2024 state nonvoter samples have Kish effective sizes of only about 13 (MI), 6 (WI) and 8 (PA). No state population confidence intervals or aligned state nonvoter population totals are available here. Thresholds are point scenarios, not proof of feasible mobilization.

## Robustness and reporting

- 2016: alternative selection specifications imply allocated Democratic shares from 60.3% to 65.3% (remove COVI, exclude Utah, region controls; also active-only in 2016). Sample restrictions change the target group.
- 2024: alternative selection specifications imply allocated Democratic shares from 77.7% to 84.8% (remove COVI, exclude Utah, region controls; also active-only in 2016). Sample restrictions change the target group.

**Presentation claim:** Predicted nonvoter preferences and required participation are sensitive to selection and allocation assumptions. Abstention is a plausible electoral scenario; its causal role in both Trump victories is not established.

**Completed executions:** weighted baselines; joint binary selection; lecture two-step diagnostic; generated-regressor and state-cluster inference; cross-year tests; benchmarks; prior-vote/Sanders descriptions; state thresholds/allocation sensitivity; exclusion, region, Utah and active-status robustness; independent CDF/gradient/simulation validation.
